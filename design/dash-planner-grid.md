# Dash planner rework: precomputed routes from a grid (design, 2026-11-06)

The user's idea (idea queue item 8), written up after the design questions of 2026-11-06. **No robot code is built for it yet**; the PC simulation of Phases 1b to 1d exists (see the last section). Today's planner (`HiveRoute`: hand-placed helper corners and lane gates, swept boxes, a search at press time) stays in use until this replaces it.

## The idea
- A **grid of starting points** covers the field. When a hive-cell dash (or a Park Dash corner) is chosen, an algorithm picks ONE grid point so the robot moves in the general direction of the target.
- From that grid point a set of **precomputed optimal paths** (a tree: the routes overlap and merge) leads to the launch location. **No collision calculation on the fly.** A hive cell may have several launch locations later; at first one each, the current preferred spots (`AutoLaunch`, x +-55.4).
- The routes are computed **offline**, with as much simulation as is useful: a good **collision simulation** (the real footprint against the supports, flower stands and walls) and a **simulation of the robot's motion dynamics**, derived from the many logs.

## Decisions (the user, 2026-11-06)
1. **The robot drives to the chosen grid point first**, then follows the tree (so the precomputed routes are never disturbed by an arbitrary start).
2. **The whole field is covered.** A start that already counts as collided (inside a flower stand or a hive support foot) does not have to be handled.
3. The same machinery serves the **Hive Dash and the Park Dash** (four launch spots, four park corners).
4. The tables are **generated by a one-command tool on a PC** and checked into the repo as generated source.
5. The **acceptance gate**: replay the logged dashes in the simulation and require it to reproduce them (including the 2026-11-06 corner at (36, 40) that swung 17 in wide into the audience flower). Later, with logs of the real code running these tables, verify the approach further.

## Proposed shape (for the user to correct)
- **State:** position on a grid (pitch about 6 in) and a few headings per point (8 or 16), or a state lattice with motion primitives. A circle of radius 12 in cannot reach the audience spot (its footprint is 6.8 in from the flower), so the heading-aware footprint is needed near flowers.
- **Tree:** per goal, a shortest-path tree rooted at the goal (Dijkstra over the grid, cost = time, not length), each point stores its next point and the speed to carry. The tree is the data structure; overlap is automatic.
- **Picking the grid point:** the point minimizing (distance to it + its cost-to-go) among points reachable by a straight, collision-free drive that does not turn away from the target.
- **Cost = time** from the dynamics model, with a corner speed limit and a clearance constraint that includes the worst observed tracking error.
- **Runtime:** a lookup, a drive to the grid point, then the route with its speed profile, with a cheap **deviation guard** (abort or re-snap when the robot is more than N in from the route), since pushes and drift still happen.
- **Generation:** a Java tool in `tools/` (the geometry already compiles on a PC: `HiveRoute`, `DuoPathCheck`), producing a generated table in `TeamCode`, with checks in the style of `tools/hive/run-check.cmd`.

## Phases
1. **Simulation and replay (the foundation).** Collision model, drive model identified from the logs, and the follower code running against the model; replay every logged dash and compare tracks. Useful on its own: it tunes the current planner's corner speeds.
2. **Measure what a grid would add** over the current planner (time, clearance) on the replayed field.
3. **Generate the tables** and a Hive Dash / Park Dash that uses them; test on the robot; compare the new logs with the simulation.

## First measurements (2026-11-06, SUPERSEDED by the second pass below; `134` dash segments from the 11-05 and 11-06 TeleOp logs, 3174 sample rows)
Fitted by least squares to the logged commands and poses (a first-order lag per axis with a dead zone, separate time constants for speeding up and slowing down):
- **Forward:** steady speed about 38 in/s at 0.5 power and 64 in/s at 0.85; fitted gain 86 in/s per unit of power above a dead zone of 0.12; time constant 0.28 s speeding up and 0.15 s slowing down; rms error 8.8 in/s (about 14 percent of top speed).
- **Strafe:** gain 33 in/s per unit (poorly determined: the logged dashes mostly drive along the heading); time constant 0.12 s; rms 6.2 in/s.
- **Turn:** gain 5.3 rad/s per unit of power, time constant about 0.12 s; rms 0.57 rad/s.
- Commands of 0.2 and 0.3 produce no motion at all (static friction): this is why a leg stalls 2 to 3 in short of its target near the end (the proportional power there is below the dead zone), as seen at the Solo's launch spot 1.
These numbers are a first pass: the rows are 50 to 100 ms apart, the speeds come from pose differences, and the commanded powers are not corrected for the wheel-power scaling when translation and turn add past 1. The simulation needs a better identification (burst-logged launches have 10 ms rows) before it can be trusted for corners.

## Phase 1a, second pass: the drive model (2026-11-06, `tools/dash/identify_drive.py`)
A per-axis model fitted for MULTI-STEP prediction: windows of 8 log rows (0.7 s), the initial velocity taken from the first step, the error being the predicted pose against the logged pose at every later row. The command is first scaled the way `MecanumDrive.setDrivePowers` scales it (the wheel powers `x - y - w` and so on, divided by the largest when it passes 1), then each axis (forward, strafe, turn) is a first-order lag towards `gain * (battery / 12 V) * sign(c) * max(0, |c| - dead zone)`, with separate time constants for speeding up and slowing down.

| Axis | Gain (all driving / dashes only) | Dead zone | tau up | tau down |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forward | 86 / 99 in/s per unit | 0.00 / 0.11 | 0.38 / 0.30 s | 0.17 / 0.22 s |
| Strafe | 63 / 52 in/s per unit | 0.05 / 0.02 | 0.31 / 0.25 s | 0.12 / 0.22 s |
| Turn | 6.3 / 6.7 rad/s per unit | 0.04 / 0.07 | 0.07 / 0.20 s | 0.10 / 0.11 s |

Accuracy (17,369 windows of all TeleOp driving, 1,725 of dashes only): doing nothing leaves 8.5 to 12 in of pose error within a window, the model 2.1 to 2.6 in rms. After 0.46 s the position error is a median 1.5 to 1.8 in (90th percentile 4.2 to 4.7 in); after 0.81 s a median 2.8 to 3.7 in (90th 7.8 to 8.4 in). **The heading is the weak part**: median 2.6 to 3.1 deg after 0.46 s and 5 to 6 deg after 0.81 s, with a 90th percentile of 19 to 30 deg during the hard turns. The model of the single steps (a plain per-step fit) stopped at about 0.74 in per 100 ms step whatever the nonlinearity added (an exponent, a static threshold), so the floor is the data: the logged command is a snapshot every 100 ms of a command that changes every 30 ms loop, and the dash fits ignore that the follower and the lock add their own turn commands within the loop.

**What this means for the simulation.** It is good enough to show a corner swing of about 17 in with an error of a few inches, so it can rank routes and corner speeds, but not yet to prove a clearance of 2 to 3 in: until the model is better the clearance margins must carry a tracking-error allowance of at least the 90th percentile figures above. Ways to improve it: log the commands every loop during dashes (a burst log, 30 ms), put the real follower in the loop (it corrects some of the model's error the way it corrects the robot's), and refit with the logs of the new code.

## Phases 1b to 1d: the simulation and the replay (2026-11-06, `tools/dash/DashSim.java`; built, NOT committed)
Run `tools\dash\run-sim.cmd` from the repo root (one command: compiles and replays every logged dash, about 10 s). Other modes: `run-sim.cmd plant dash` (the drive model alone), `run-sim.cmd run -65 50 0 BLUE_AUDIENCE` (one dash with today's values), `--case 2026-11-06/004300:182.5` (one logged dash with a trace), `--tracks DIR` (CSV tracks). The options are in the file's header.

**What it is.**
- **1b, the harness.** The REAL `HiveDash`, `ParkDash`, `HoldPosition`, `TargetLock`, `HiveRoute`, `AutoLaunch` (and `HiveTarget`, `PidController`, `math.*`) compiled on a PC with stubs in `tools/dash/stubs`: `ElapsedTime` on a simulated clock, a `RunLog` that collects the events, a no-op `@Config` and Dashboard overlay. No robot file was changed. The loop is `TeleOpCompetition`'s glue: the Park Dash, then the Hive Dash, the dash's translation, the target lock's turn when the dash wants the lock (else the dash's turn), a salvo of 1.3 s on `launchNow`, `HoldPosition` during a salvo or at a park corner. The loop time is each dash's logged loop time (about 31 ms). The Pinpoint is the true pose plus 0.1 in / 0.1 deg of noise (a delay is an option; 30 ms made no difference). The plant is `identify_drive.py`'s model ported to Java; `plant` mode reproduces the Python figures to the printed digit (1,725 dash windows, 17,369 of all driving).
- **1c, the collision model.** The footprint (17.48 x 13.70 in, origin 7.6 in from the back) at each pose against the two foot bars, the four A-frame legs at 18 in, the four flower stands (the CAD rectangles of `HiveRoute`) and the walls, as a signed clearance every 10 ms, for the simulated AND the logged tracks. The walls also stop the simulated robot (a robot pressed against one otherwise drove through it).
- **1d, the replay.** Every `HIVE DASH start` / `PARK DASH start` in the 11-05 and 11-06 TeleOp logs: 130 dashes in 7 logs (`200305`, `205206`, `213945`, `000512`, `004300`, `011331`, `015817`; `212455` has none). Each starts at its logged pose with the run's own Dashboard values (its `CONFIG` lines: power 0.5 in `200305`, launch spots at x +-61.5 before `004300`); a `HiveDash` feature the run did not have yet is set off (`APPROACH_POWER`, `CORNER_POWER`, `EARLY_LOCK_RADIUS_IN` 0, `LANE_HALF_WIDTH_IN` 22.75, `FLOWER_CLEARANCE_IN` 0, `LAUNCH_HOLD_SEC` 0.15). The start velocity comes from running the plant 0.3 s on the logged commands before the press (the plain difference of the two rows before it was up to 0.1 s stale: a robot braking hard started 35 in/s too fast). The D-pad is released at the logged cancel. When today's planner plans another route than the log had (10 dashes: the early runs had no flower keep-outs) the logged route is put into the real dash by reflection. Excluded (11): released within 0.3 s, a battery below 9 V.
- **The gate**, per dash: the median track difference at most 4 in (until the first logged contact: the plant has no contact forces); the arrival point within 3 in; the overshoot (the largest distance from the spot in the 0.8 s after the arrival) within 3 in; for routes with via points, the largest distance off the route within 5 in (the corner swing); the same set of contacts.

**What the replay showed about the plant.** The identified model alone (`--plant fitted`) passes 82 of 130 and misses the corners by 5 to 8 in. The logged corners show why:
1. **Momentum.** At the helper corner (36, 40) the robot arrives at 75 in/s and goes on about 20 in in +x while it turns 120 deg (205206 at 180.8 s). The model's velocity lag acts in the robot's frame, so the velocity turns with the robot. Now: the lag acts on the field velocity, with a **grip limit** on how fast that velocity can change.
2. **Turning while rolling.** The robot turned at up to 320 deg/s on a 0.5 turn command at speed; the model gives about 180 deg/s, which is right from rest. Now: the turn gain times (1 + 1.5 x speed / 60 in/s).
3. **The battery reading.** The logged volts (refreshed once a second, sagging under load) put the simulated robot 10 in behind before the corner. Now: a fixed 12.5 V.

These three extras were chosen on the 11-05 logs only (44 dashes; a grid over grip 90 to 220 in/s^2, boost 0.8 to 3, 12.0 to 13.0 V) and then checked on the 11-06 logs (86 dashes, not used for the choice). The result is the default, `--plant tuned` (dash fit, field frame, grip 150 in/s^2, boost 1.5, 12.5 V):

| held-out 11-06 dashes | fitted (dash) | fitted (all driving) | tuned |
|---|---|---|---|
| pass the whole gate | 51 of 86 | 56 | 52 |
| median track difference <= 4 in | 60 | 67 | 68 |
| corner swing within 5 in (32 with via points) | 24 | 24 | 27 |
| mean corner swing error | 3.5 in | 3.2 in | 2.9 in |
| arrival time, sim - log (median) | +0.18 s | +0.15 s | -0.01 s |
| overshoot within 3 in (60 arrivals) | 50 | 50 | 53 |
| arrival point within 3 in (60 arrivals) | 53 | 54 | 49 |

All 130 with the tuned plant: **89 pass** (fitted dash 82, fitted all 88); median track difference within 4 in for 110, corner swing within 5 in for 42 of 47, overshoot within 3 in for 76 of 84, arrival within 3 in for 70 of 84 (arrival time sim - log median -0.01 s, 10th to 90th percentile -0.47 to +0.16 s), the same contacts for 123. **The two 11-06 dashes from (-65, 50) via (36, 40)** (004300 at 182.5 and 265.8 s): swing 12.9 and 11.6 in simulated against 14.7 and 14.3 logged, and the audience flower contact found in both. The 11-05 one (205206 at 180.8 s) swings 12.3 against 17.2 and fails.

**The gate is not met (89 of 130).** Where it still disagrees:
- **Braking at zero power against coasting at part power.** A straight stop at zero power slows from 67 in/s to 0 in about 0.25 s (015817 at 13.3 s, over 250 in/s^2) while the robot in a corner, on part power, slows at about 110 in/s^2. One grip limit cannot do both (150 brakes the straight stops too gently). The likely cause is the motor driver: a wheel commanded below its speed but not to zero coasts, at zero it brakes (BRAKE mode). The next model is per wheel, with drive / coast / brake modes, fitted again from the logs.
- **Arrival point within 3 in** (14 fail, by 3.0 to 4.7 in): the arrival is the first loop inside 2.5 in, so two approaches from slightly different directions arrive up to 5 in apart.
- **The big overshoots** (6 to 11 in logged, 000512 at 256.9, 353.9 and 629.7 s) are simulated 3 to 5 in too small.
- **The data**: 100 ms rows of a command that changes every 30 ms. Logging every loop during a dash (`RunLog.setBurst` exists) would give the follower's real commands; that is a robot change and costs loop time, so it is the user's decision.

**A finding about today's Hive Dash** (independent of the rework): the turn at the lane gate (-34, 0) at speed swings under the hive into the red supports. Logged: 015817 at 8.5 s went through the gate at 65 in/s and out to y -14.2, and the collision model finds the red legs and foot bar touched. Simulated with today's values from (-65, 50, 0 deg) to the audience cell: the same route, out to y -13, the red rear leg touched by 2.0 in. The corner slowdown only takes that 58 deg turn down to 0.66 power.

**What it is good for now:** ranking routes and corner speeds and checking clearances with a tracking allowance of about 5 in (the 90th percentile track difference). Not yet for proving a 2 to 3 in clearance.

## Drive characterization (2026-11-06; built, NOT run on the robot yet)
The logs are a poor base for the plant (100 ms rows of a command that changes every 30 ms, and match driving rarely isolates one effect), so a purpose-built opmode drives test patterns by itself and logs every loop. **Every log from 2026-11-05 on is on the current drivetrain** (the belts were tightened before then so the pulleys do not slip; user, 2026-11-06): the fits and the replay above used only those logs. Values measured from the older logs are suspect: `HiveDash.UNALIGNED_SPEED_RATIO` 0.75 (11-01 to 11-03) and the turn response in `TargetLock`'s notes.

- **Opmode** `Drive Characterization` (Tuning group; `opmodes/tuning/DriveCharacterization.java`), the logic in `drive/DriveTestRoutine.java` (pure Java: the simulation runs the same class). Constraints from the user: half a field (each alliance's half, split at the center line) and 5 minutes per session; the drive motor encoders are not wired, so the Pinpoint is the only speed signal.
- **Safety.** The driver HOLDS the right bumper (a deadman): letting go zeroes the powers (the motors brake), holding it again restarts the current test from its start. X skips a test. A geofence stops a run when the footprint, or the footprint where the robot would stop at 200 in/s^2 (the far-corner square while it turns fast), would cross a wall (68 in), the center line (y 4 in), the hive (|x| 22.5, y 28) or a flower grown 3 in. No mechanism moves.
- **Where.** A lane along x at y = 46 (blue frame; red is turned 180 deg), from x -54 to +54; spots at x +-40. Each run goes from the end the robot is nearer to the other end, so one test starts where the last ended. Turns, direction changes and diagonals go toward the center line late in the run, past the hive's end (about 30 in of room there, about 14 toward the wall).
- **The tests (34, most important first):** stops from 0.85 and 0.6 (brake at zero, coast on 0.2, drop to 0.45, reverse 0.4; front, rear and strafing), turning while rolling (0.5 and 0.3 for 0.25 to 0.3 s, and zero power plus a turn), direction changes at speed (50 deg, and a Hive Dash corner command), steady speed (forward 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 / 1.0, strafe 0.4 / 0.7 / 1.0, a short diagonal), spins (0.3 to 1.0), the dead zone (creeping at 0.08 / 0.12 / 0.16 forward, strafe, 0.05 / 0.08 / 0.12 turn), and two random sequences as a check.
- **The log** `drivetest.log` (design/run-logs.md): a row on every loop with the pose, the Pinpoint's field velocity, the command and the four wheel powers sent, the battery every loop and one motor current per loop in turn (the current shows whether a wheel on part power brakes or coasts).
- **Simulated** (`tools\dash\run-sim.cmd drivetest`, the tuned plant at a 15 ms loop): all 34 tests in 163 s (2.7 min), no contact, the least clearance 3.4 in to a support and 4.2 in to a flower, never past the center line; the geofence stopped the last random test. With other plants (the plain fits, grip 100 or 250, gains +15 %, a 25 ms loop) it is 2.6 to 2.7 min, never a contact, and 0 to 4 tests stopped by the geofence (stops of the coast and reverse tests near the lane's end, and the random ones).
- **Next, after a session:** fit the model from `drivetest.log` (a per-wheel drive / coast / brake model; `identify_drive.py` reads the TeleOp layout and needs a reader for the new columns), then replay the dashes again.
