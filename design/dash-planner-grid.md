# Dash planner rework: precomputed routes from a grid (design, 2026-11-06)

The user's idea (idea queue item 8), written up after the design questions of 2026-11-06. **Nothing here is built.** Today's planner (`HiveRoute`: hand-placed helper corners and lane gates, swept boxes, a search at press time) stays in use until this replaces it.

## The idea
- A **grid of starting points** covers the field. When a hive-cell dash (or a Park Dash corner) is chosen, an algorithm picks ONE grid point so the robot moves in the general direction of the target.
- From that grid point a set of **precomputed optimal paths** (a tree: the routes overlap and merge) leads to the launch location. **No collision calculation on the fly.** A hive cell may have several launch locations later; at first one each, the current preferred spots (`AutoLaunch`, x +-55.4).
- The routes are computed **offline**, with as much simulation as is useful: a good **collision simulation** (the real footprint against the supports, flower stands and walls) and a **simulation of the robot's motion dynamics**, derived from the many logs.

## Decisions (the user, 2026-11-06)
1. **The robot drives to the chosen grid point first**, then follows the tree (so the precomputed routes are never disturbed by an arbitrary start).
2. **The whole field is covered.** A start that already counts as collided (inside a flower stand or a hive support foot) does not have to be handled.
3. The same machinery serves the **Hive Dash and the Park Dash** (four launch spots, four park corners).
4. The tables are **generated by a one-command tool on a PC** and checked into the repo as generated source.
5. The **acceptance gate**: replay the logged dashes in the simulation and require it to reproduce them (including the 2026-11-06 corner at (36, 40) that swung 17 in wide into the audience flower). Later, with logs of the real code running these tables, verify the approach further.

## Proposed shape (for the user to correct)
- **State:** position on a grid (pitch about 6 in) and a few headings per point (8 or 16), or a state lattice with motion primitives. A circle of radius 12 in cannot reach the audience spot (its footprint is 6.8 in from the flower), so the heading-aware footprint is needed near flowers.
- **Tree:** per goal, a shortest-path tree rooted at the goal (Dijkstra over the grid, cost = time, not length), each point stores its next point and the speed to carry. The tree is the data structure; overlap is automatic.
- **Picking the grid point:** the point minimizing (distance to it + its cost-to-go) among points reachable by a straight, collision-free drive that does not turn away from the target.
- **Cost = time** from the dynamics model, with a corner speed limit and a clearance constraint that includes the worst observed tracking error.
- **Runtime:** a lookup, a drive to the grid point, then the route with its speed profile, with a cheap **deviation guard** (abort or re-snap when the robot is more than N in from the route), since pushes and drift still happen.
- **Generation:** a Java tool in `tools/` (the geometry already compiles on a PC: `HiveRoute`, `DuoPathCheck`), producing a generated table in `TeamCode`, with checks in the style of `tools/hive/run-check.cmd`.

## Phases
1. **Simulation and replay (the foundation).** Collision model, drive model identified from the logs, and the follower code running against the model; replay every logged dash and compare tracks. Useful on its own: it tunes the current planner's corner speeds.
2. **Measure what a grid would add** over the current planner (time, clearance) on the replayed field.
3. **Generate the tables** and a Hive Dash / Park Dash that uses them; test on the robot; compare the new logs with the simulation.

## First measurements (2026-11-06, `134` dash segments from the 11-05 and 11-06 TeleOp logs, 3174 sample rows)
Fitted by least squares to the logged commands and poses (a first-order lag per axis with a dead zone, separate time constants for speeding up and slowing down):
- **Forward:** steady speed about 38 in/s at 0.5 power and 64 in/s at 0.85; fitted gain 86 in/s per unit of power above a dead zone of 0.12; time constant 0.28 s speeding up and 0.15 s slowing down; rms error 8.8 in/s (about 14 percent of top speed).
- **Strafe:** gain 33 in/s per unit (poorly determined: the logged dashes mostly drive along the heading); time constant 0.12 s; rms 6.2 in/s.
- **Turn:** gain 5.3 rad/s per unit of power, time constant about 0.12 s; rms 0.57 rad/s.
- Commands of 0.2 and 0.3 produce no motion at all (static friction): this is why a leg stalls 2 to 3 in short of its target near the end (the proportional power there is below the dead zone), as seen at the Solo's launch spot 1.
These numbers are a first pass: the rows are 50 to 100 ms apart, the speeds come from pose differences, and the commanded powers are not corrected for the wheel-power scaling when translation and turn add past 1. The simulation needs a better identification (burst-logged launches have 10 ms rows) before it can be trusted for corners.
